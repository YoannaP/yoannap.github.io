I have always been very "into" books. Not just reading them, but reading about what people have read and how that has influenced them. I love organising my books, into themes, colour coded, depends on how I feel. I listen to books. I read them on the Kindle. I always buy the physical copy. I love hanging out in book stores, even just to browse, not for the purpose of reading or buying something. I don't think I love reading per se, I love the idea of books as a whole.

My bookshelf at home is a mess. It has ten thousand rows and stacked books on top of these rows (we are moving soon so that will no longer be the case when we expand the library).

I've had a lot of time to think about what I want to do to tidy up my book mental load now that I have a baby because 1) I walk him for about 3 hours a day so can listen to Audible and 2) I breastfeed him for about 6 hours a day so can use my Kindle. After binging on multiple Desperate Housewives seasons and ordering thousands of things my baby ABSOLUTELY needs, I decided my brain is fried so I need to do something productive during breastfeeding and walking - reading seemed like a good idea.

As I was deciding what to read and thinking about what I have enjoyed in the past, I thought, how great would it be that now that I have my Obsidian vault, I can access everything I have read across all platforms, but also I consolidate all the random want-to-read notes from notes, Goodreads, my website ... my at-home bookshelf?

This led to the [shelf-scanner](https://github.com/YoannaP/shelf-scanner) app, the app which scans a shelf and asks me to classify each book as a read, want-to-read, did not finish and skip. That was just awesome because I could immediately see how much I have read this year and give myself a pat on the back that even though I feel like I have had 0 time in the last 2 months (you know, baby duty), I still have a good rate of 1 book per week - pat, pat.

Once I give the app a thumbs up that the books look correctly classified, it channels them to my Obsidian vault which can be synced with my Goodreads. Similarly, my Kindle is also synced with my Goodreads account so I can choose books easily from there. Any books that I love are of course added to my [Top Shelf](/books/) when scanned by the app.

A nice plus is that you can scan any shelf - so when I visit people's homes and I inspect their bookshelves (another thing I love doing) you can scan and easily add to want to read, which will drop into your Goodreads account.

I'm still figuring out the workflow of book save, read, record, add to Top Shelf as this is new for me but my plan is for this to be a small part of the personal agent I'm working towards to unload myself from the massive workload which comes as part of being a mother.
