I measure how impactful books are by: how it made me feel, how much did I follow up with other readings from it, how much did I enjoy reading it. This is an overall feel.  


**Most impactful:** 

- War and Peace by Leo Tolstoy
Because it is an extremely detailed account of the Napoleonic Wars which inspires you to learn more about the period. At the same time it shows you what it means to be human. 

- Catherine the Great: Portrait of a woman by Robert K Massie 
Because it shows you an extremely powerful woman, who went through hardship to achieve incredible things for Russia, even though she was not Russian. Shows her incredible motivation to overthrow her husband despite being very hated at the beginning and really not liked by the reigning monarch Elizabeth. 

- Measure what matters by John Doer 
Because it inspired me to start doing personal OKRs and sports betting OKRs for some time. It didn't last a very long time because a shorter rhythm felt more appropriate. On the personal side, I found it more useful to follow interests than to follow a set goal especially because so many new things are coming out every day. 

- Abundance
Because it touches on very current issues in an eloquent way e.g. about housing crisis. Perhaps a little bit too America focused. 

- Selfish reasons to have more kids by Bryan Kaplan 
Because it completely changed my opinion on whether or not you should have children early on or not. As a general rule of thumb, you will most likely end up having less children than you actually want and later than you want them. People tend to benefit from having more children because they underestimate the long-term benefits and over estimate the short term disadvantages such as money struggles, sleepless nights and restrictions in nightlife. 

- Henry Ford’s autobiography
Because it's a rare account of how he saw himself (probably a little bit fake but we can ignore this) as well as other tycoons at the time like JP Morgan. Similar vibe to letters written by JD Rockefeller to his son. A lot of learnings about how he though through scaling his business. 

- Boom: bubbles and the end of stagnation
Because it distinguishes between good and bad bubbles rather than lumping everything together. It also includes so many references to other resources, I spent about the same amount of time following up on references as I did reading the book. The Stripe edition is beautiful and all the references are at the bottom of the page rather than at the back so they are easy to follow up on.
