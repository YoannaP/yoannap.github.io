Listened to Patrick O'Shaughnessy and David Senra having a conversation about Senra reading different biographies. He has a good perspective on people who have been successful. Unsurprisingly, he mentions that a common denominator in a lot of these biographies is focus and perseverance.

Often I catch myself being impatient and wanting to shortcut difficult situations - example: "This strategy I tried didn't work so nothing works"

I often aim for short term gains, which is exasperated by setting unrealistically high expectations (even more so now with AI) and I have evolved into being a lot more impatient than I used to be. I think this is a general point about the world nowadays and I see it around unfortunately - raising before showing profits, registering the company before building anything, giving up after a few mistakes, failures or criticisms, starting something and not following through.

The person who I admire most is the anti-example to these things. He has the utmost sense of perseverance and focus and nothing stops him from working on the problem he is focusing on. He doesn't think: "wait I need to fill up my water bottle every 30 mins", or "I cannot work on this chair because it's not an office chair" or "I only have 20 mins which isn't enough to get this done".

I find this mentality very inspiring as it also helps you be in the moment more, stops you from rushing things as you're looking to just chip away at a problem continuously and keeps you focused on that one important thing. I'd like to be more like this.
